\begin{abstract}
Bus timetable planning and real-time holding control are operationally coupled yet typically addressed in isolation. We present TransitDuet, a bi-level reinforcement learning (RL) framework that jointly optimizes rolling dispatch timetables and station-level holding decisions under stochastic demand and traffic conditions. The upper policy selects discrete planned-headway adjustments that define a dynamic rolling timetable, while the lower policy, a discrete robust ensemble soft actor-critic (RE-SAC) controller with Lagrangian headway constraints, executes the timetable through station-level holding. Cross-level coordination combines the dynamic timetable channel with holding-feedback state augmentation and per-dispatch hindsight credit assignment; measurement-driven reward weighting adaptively penalizes fleet overshoot.

We evaluate TransitDuet in a calibrated bidirectional bus-corridor microsimulation with stochastic demand, traffic, and elastic fleet budgets. The evaluation reports passenger waiting time, headway regularity, fleet overshoot, holding time, onboard time, total passenger time, dispatch lateness, and planned-versus-realized timetable variability. To isolate the value of timetable optimization, we compare the timetable policy against a target-headway-only variant, fixed-headway soft actor-critic (SAC) baselines, independent upper--lower assembly, classical holding rules, and same-simulator RL and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) proxy baselines. Across ten independently trained seeds, each assessed on 50 held-out stochastic episodes, the final rolling-timetable policy achieves a composite cost of 1.431, 2.4\% lower than the target-headway-only variant (1.467), 6.2\% lower than the fixed-timetable SAC baseline with a 300\,s timetable (1.525), and 4.1\% lower than the same-simulator RL proxy (1.492), while reducing total passenger time to 16.15 minutes.
\end{abstract}
