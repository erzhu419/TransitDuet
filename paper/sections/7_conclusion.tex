\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

TransitDuet is a bi-level reinforcement learning framework for dynamic bus timetable optimization and real-time holding control.
The upper policy selects rolling planned-headway adjustments for future dispatches, which the simulator converts into planned launch times. The lower policy regulates holding actions at station arrivals. These tasks are operationally coupled but are often addressed separately.

The dynamic timetable channel (Section~\ref{sec:tap}) links the two levels. The lower Lagrangian cost is evaluated against the planned headway associated with its dispatched trip.
Operational constraints are handled through complementary mechanisms: an online gradient descent update reweights the upper-level fleet-overshoot penalty, while Lagrangian relaxation maintains headway regularity at the lower level.

The experiments report both service-quality metrics and timetable-execution metrics, including waiting time, headway CV, fleet overshoot, holding time, onboard time, total passenger time, planned timetable variability, and dispatch lateness. A target-headway-only model is retained as an ablation to isolate the contribution of directly optimizing planned dispatch times.

Future work should test TransitDuet on multi-route networks with shared fleets and transfer coordination. Deployment through a calibrated digital twin with domain randomization could assess robustness to traffic signals and driver heterogeneity. Real-time demand forecasting and passenger-demand elasticity would further connect service quality to ridership.
